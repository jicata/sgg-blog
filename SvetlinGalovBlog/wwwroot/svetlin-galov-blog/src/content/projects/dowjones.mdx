<Section label="What I did">

Built and maintained internal platform services powering the editorial and data pipelines at Dow Jones. Worked across distributed systems in a large-scale engineering org — multiple teams, longer review cycles, and a production blast radius that demanded rigor at every layer.

<TextCard bulletNumber={"01"} heading={"Distributed messaging and event pipelines"}>
  Designed and extended event-driven pipelines using RabbitMQ and AWS SQS. Focused on reliability patterns: dead-letter queues, idempotent consumers, and ordered delivery guarantees for high-throughput editorial workflows.
</TextCard>

<TextCard bulletNumber={"02"} heading={"Internal platform services in .NET Core"}>
  Built REST and gRPC services that served downstream editorial tooling. Worked closely with the platform team to align on contracts, versioning, and observability standards across a multi-team codebase.
</TextCard>

<TextCard bulletNumber={"03"} heading={"AWS infrastructure and deployment"}>
  Participated in infrastructure work on AWS — ECS task definitions, parameter store, CloudWatch alarms. Understood how deployment pipelines operate at org scale with multiple environments and approval gates.
</TextCard>

</Section>

<Section label="What I learned">

Working at Dow Jones scale changed how I think about production systems. At a startup you can push fast and fix fast. At a global media company, a bad deploy touches thousands of editorial workflows in markets you can't easily reach. That raised the bar on how I approach testing, change management, and system contracts.

The most durable lesson: at scale, the bottleneck is rarely the code — it's coordination, shared understanding, and clear ownership. Good engineers at large orgs spend as much time on those as on the implementation.

</Section>
