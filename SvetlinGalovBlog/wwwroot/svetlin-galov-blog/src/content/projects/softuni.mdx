<Section label="What I did">

Spent four years split between building software and teaching it. Designed and delivered the C# and .NET backend curriculum at SoftUni, one of Bulgaria's largest software education programs. Alongside classroom work, built and maintained internal tooling supporting the platform's content operations.

<TextCard bulletNumber={"01"} heading={"Curriculum design and classroom delivery"}>
  Designed the C# and .NET backend track — syllabus, exercises, assessments, live coding sessions. Taught hundreds of students going from zero to their first backend role. The constraint of explaining things clearly to people who don't share your mental model is one of the best ways to stress-test your own understanding.
</TextCard>

<TextCard bulletNumber={"02"} heading={"Internal tooling and platform support"}>
  Built and maintained internal administrative tooling for the SoftUni platform in .NET Framework. Small team, direct feedback loops, full ownership of the features I shipped.
</TextCard>

</Section>

<Section label="What I learned">

Teaching forced a level of conceptual clarity that pure engineering work doesn't require. When a student asks why something works a certain way and "because that's the convention" doesn't land, you have to go deeper. That habit stuck — I still tend to explain decisions rather than assert them, whether I'm reviewing a PR or introducing an architecture to a new team.

The other lesson: the best engineers I've worked with since are often the ones who can explain their systems simply. SoftUni gave me early training in that direction.

</Section>
